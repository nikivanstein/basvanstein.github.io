abstract: >-
  Current research into remaining useful life (RUL) has a strong emphasis on single-component or
  simple multi-component systems. As such, it often fails to account for the interactions between
  components and systems, as well as the impact of high fluctuations in operating conditions. The
  existing body of literature on RUL is therefore only marginally applicable to researchers and asset
  managers dealing with complex, multi-component systems, such as cars, trains, or ships. At the same
  time, the substantial costs involved in acquiring and maintaining these complex systems make
  optimising maintenance management through a condition-based approach a potentially valuable avenue
  for improving life-cycle performance. In this paper, we firstly define and clarify the distinction
  between simple and complex multi-component systems. Secondly, we explore the consequences of this
  distinction for RUL estimation. Next, we review existing literature on RUL in the context of complex
  systems, and finally, we distil opportunities and gaps for further research into such systems. Our
  goal is to provide a reference point for more comprehensive research and better application
  development in the domain of RUL calculations for complex systems.
